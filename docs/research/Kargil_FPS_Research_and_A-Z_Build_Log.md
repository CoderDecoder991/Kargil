**Working title:** *Heights of 1999* (temporary)

**Format:** Windows single player first person shooter; the first playable mission follows a documented 4 JAT patrol in the Kargil conflict.

**Mission 1 update:** The first playable mission now follows Lt. Saurabh Kalia and the five soldiers of his 4 JAT patrol. ## 1. Historical foundation

The conflict followed Pakistani infiltration across the Line of Control into commanding heights in the Kargil region in early 1999. Local observers reported suspicious movement on **3 May 1999**; Indian patrols confirmed intruders in the following days. Indian operations to evict them became Operation Vijay. The Indian Air Force joined on 26 May. The Battle of Tololing was a major breakthrough in June, Tiger Hill was recaptured in July, and 26 July is commemorated as Kargil Vijay Diwas. [Kargil Review Committee executive summary](https://www.nuclearweaponarchive.org/~nuclearw/India/KargilRCC.html); [Indian Ministry of Defence overview](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2288840&lang=1&reg=3).

**Important correction for the opening:** The official review describes infiltrators moving through gaps between winter positions and initially avoiding confrontation. Heavy snow led to the evacuation of one named post, **Bajrang**, in March. A scene showing a large assault that kills the Indian guards of a manned post would be misleading if presented as the actual opening of the war. A credible cinematic can show a *composite* group of Pakistani personnel and irregular fighters crossing a ridge, taking an empty shelter or unheld height, building stone cover, and observing the valley below. The exact people and actions in that particular scene must be labelled dramatized. [Kargil Review Committee](https://www.nuclearweaponarchive.org/~nuclearw/India/KargilRCC.html).

**Composition needs careful wording.** The Review Committee says Pakistan portrayed most intruders as mujahideen, while its assessment found substantial participation by Northern Light Infantry personnel; the ratio of regulars to irregulars was disputed. A scholarly examination also notes the dispute. We can depict both categories in a composite prologue, but must not claim a verified unit mix at a specific post without stronger evidence. [Kargil Review Committee](https://www.nuclearweaponarchive.org/~nuclearw/India/KargilRCC.html); [Cambridge University Press, *Asymmetric Warfare in South Asia*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/asymmetric-warfare-in-south-asia/militants-in-the-kargil-conflict-myths-realities-and-impacts/366480B8C5D7111E98EC7B67B4059FC6).

The occupied heights overlooked the Srinagar–Leh highway. Indian assaults faced steep approaches, thin air, cold, a defender above the attacker, and major logistics challenges. The Review Committee records difficulty with cold-weather clothing and infantry load. The Ministry of Defence describes near-vertical climbing and battles above 5,000 metres. [Review Committee](https://www.nuclearweaponarchive.org/~nuclearw/India/KargilRCC.html); [Ministry of Defence overview](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2288840&lang=1&reg=3).

**What is established vs dramatized**

| Established historical frame | Dramatic construction for the game |
|---|---|
| Infiltration preceded the May discovery; a local report came on 3 May. | Individual conversations, the specific group seen in the prologue, and its exact route. |
| At least one named post, Bajrang, was vacated after heavy snow; infiltrators also occupied unheld gaps and heights. | An unnamed empty ridge shelter used for the cinematic, so no false claim that a particular defended post was stormed. |
| Regular Pakistani personnel were substantially involved; the role and proportion of irregular fighters remain debated. | Showing both groups in one composite scene, clearly identified as a dramatization. |
| Indian soldiers fought under severe altitude, cold, terrain, and load constraints. | A fictional squad and objective that teach those constraints without claiming to recreate a real unit's actions. |

## 2. Opening cinematic treatment

**Prologue, late winter 1999 — 90–120 seconds, provisional.** No exact real post name is used.

1. Wide shot: the ridgeline dwarfs both sides. Wind strips snow from exposed rock; a distant highway is barely visible.
2. A small mixed group crosses a narrow, unheld gap at night. Faces, insignia, and exact unit claims are withheld pending further source work. They shelter during a whiteout, then reach an empty stone position. No invented massacre.
3. At dawn they reinforce a rough stone position. The camera establishes the vertical distance to the road: the height itself is the threat.
4. Cut to early May. A local observer notices unnatural movement on a ridge and reports it. The story then cuts to 4 JAT in Kaksar and Lt. Kalia's patrol briefing. These are separate sectors and events; the edit must not imply that his final patrol was sent in direct response to that one report.
5. Title card: “Inspired by documented events of the 1999 Kargil conflict. Characters and this opening sequence are dramatized.”

**Tone:** tense, restrained, human, and cinematic. The prologue should explain geography and surprise before spectacle. Historical text and dates appear only where sourced.

## 3. The mountain is the main opponent

| Challenge | Player experience | Representation and accessibility |
|---|---|---|
| Vertical terrain | Routes traverse scree, snow shelves, rock steps, and exposed ridges; summit progress is earned in stages. | Distinct terrain silhouettes and visible elevation gain; no endless generic staircase. |
| Thin air and exertion | Sprinting or carrying more gear raises breath and slows recovery. Acclimatization is established in the story. | A readable exertion meter and voice/breath cues; no fantasy “oxygen damage” for normal movement. Adjustable intensity. |
| Cold and exposure | Wind and long pauses at exposed spots affect steadiness and decision making. Sheltered pauses matter. | Weather affects audio and animation; avoid arbitrary instant freezing. |
| Visibility | Snow, blowing dust, darkness, and ridge shadow change what the player can see. | Weather has readable bounds; high-contrast and reduced-obscuration settings preserve playability. |
| Load and logistics | Ammunition, medical supplies, and ropes have weight and availability. Resupply is part of mission pacing. | Limited inventory choices, never a tedious survival grind. |
| Navigation | Landmarks, compass bearings, squad callouts, and a paper map guide the route. | Optional guidance mode for players who need clearer markers. |
| Human stakes | Recover a separated squadmate, communicate observations, and hold a route. | Objectives include more than eliminating enemies. |

Altitude design follows the real physiological principle that high elevation reduces performance and requires acclimatization; game parameters will be tuned for enjoyable play, not presented as a medical simulation. [U.S. Army mountain and cold-weather manual](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2023/01/31/557d66e2/20-14.pdf); [U.S. Army research summary](https://www.army.mil/article/195750/army_researchers_redefine_altitude_ascent_guidance_for_high_altitude_missions).

## 4. First complete mission: revised vertical slice

**Historical patrol:** Lt. Saurabh Kalia and five soldiers of 4 JAT move toward the Bajrang spur in Kaksar. The patrol and loss of contact are documented; the exact events within the missing period are not. The mission will identify its reconstructed terrain, dialogue, and encounter as dramatized. See the separate mission dossier for the source comparison and build plan.

**Player:** Lt. Saurabh Kalia (later remembered as Captain), with all five patrol members represented by name.  
**Length target:** 20–30 minutes when complete.  
**Playable arc:** briefing → six-man approach → demanding ascent and reconnaissance → loss of contact and an encounter presented as a reconstruction → fade before captivity → sourced aftermath and remembrance.  
**Success criterion:** The player understands the physical difficulty of the patrol and remembers all six people, while the game never claims certainty about undocumented moments.

Initial combat uses believable pacing, limited ammunition, squad communication, cover, and clear hit feedback. The historical outcome will not be turned into a score or a win condition. We will avoid copying another game's levels, characters, music, or branding.

## 5. Engine and visual plan for this PC

Local hardware measurement found an **Intel Core i5-6400** (4 cores/4 threads), **16 GB RAM**, **NVIDIA GeForce GT 730** with driver 456.71, and a **1920×1080 at 60 Hz** display on Windows 11 Pro. Godot 4.7.2 booted with **OpenGL 3.3 Compatibility**. WMI returned 4,293,918,720 bytes for adapter memory, close to the 32-bit field limit; **dedicated VRAM remains unverified**, so do not set a texture budget from that value.

**Prototype 0.1 benchmark on the actual GT 730, 20 October 2026:** a 20-second scripted camera traverse of the collidable 32,000-triangle ridge, Balanced detail, V-sync off and FPS uncapped, measured **90.5 average / 85.7 fifth-percentile FPS at 1280×720**, **69.1 / 67.5 at 1600×900**, and **49.6 / 49.1 at 1920×1080**. One captured frame briefly disturbed the lowest instantaneous sample. These figures describe this early scene only; squad, encounter, and future art can reduce performance. The initial benchmark before a collision-winding fix overstated 1080p performance and is superseded by these figures. The current preset choices are Performance 1280×720, Balanced 1600×900 (default), and High 1920×1080, all capped at 60 FPS during play. There is no finished-game FPS promise.

**Recommended first engine:** Godot 4 with its Compatibility renderer, subject to a boot-and-benchmark test. Godot documents OpenGL 3.3 as the compatibility baseline and calls for performance testing on low-end hardware. Unreal Engine 5's published recommendation is 32 GB system RAM and 8 GB graphics RAM, with newer GPUs needed for its advanced rendering features. [Godot requirements](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/about/system_requirements.html); [Unreal Engine requirements](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/hardware-and-software-specifications-for-unreal-engine).

**Art direction:** believable rock scale, high-quality silhouettes, convincing snow placement, strong sun and moon lighting, sparse vegetation, readable distances, period-appropriate props, and carefully mixed wind/footstep/radio sound. This is the route to realistic atmosphere on the GT 730.

| Preset | Intended use | Planned controls |
|---|---|---|
| Performance | This PC if needed | Lower render scale and shadows, shorter vegetation/distant detail, reduced particles. |
| Balanced | Default if benchmark supports it | Full display resolution when practical, moderate shadows and terrain detail. |
| High | Stronger GPUs or this PC if a small scene allows it | Longer view distance, higher textures and shadows, richer weather and post-processing. |
| Custom | Player choice | Resolution, render scale, texture quality, shadow quality, draw distance, effects, frame cap, V-sync, field of view. |

Do not promise a resolution or frame rate until the first terrain benchmark runs on this PC. Higher settings can exist even when this GPU cannot run them smoothly. A GPU upgrade would materially expand visual headroom; the project design should preserve that upgrade path.

**Terrain source plan:** NASA SRTM elevation data can guide broad mountain shapes, but its roughly 1 arc-second sampling cannot reproduce cliff geometry or historical post layout. Artists must sculpt the close-up climb and verify important locations separately. [NASA SRTM guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-05/SRTM_Quick_Guide.pdf?VersionId=ZDrZprebSzTuJdC57vPsRXhuXU3S1OZK).
