# Mission 1 dossier: the Bajrang patrol

**Project:** *Heights of 1999* (working title)  
**Prepared:** 20 October 2026  
**Decision:** First playable mission follows **Lt. Saurabh Kalia**, later remembered as Captain, and five soldiers of **4 JAT** in the Kaksar sector.  
**Research state:** Strong evidence for the patrol, route area, loss of contact, and handover of remains; incomplete evidence for the precise firefight, dialogue, ammunition state, and moment of capture.

## Executive decision

The mission can be a compelling first person experience, but its central truth is a reconnaissance patrol that did not return. The best design is a **20–30 minute, terrain-led patrol** that gives each soldier a presence, makes the altitude and route matter, and ends without a playable captivity or torture scene. The patrol can encounter hostile fire as a clearly labelled reconstruction. The game must not award a victory or score for prolonging the documented outcome, invent confirmed radio reports, or imply that Kalia alone discovered the whole Kargil intrusion.

## 2. The six people at the centre

Use the names as given in the [family-backed petition](https://rajeev.in/rajeev_writes/captain_kalia_unhrc_petition/torture_captain_kalia_unhrc_letter_final.pdf):

1. Lt. Saurabh Kalia, 4 JAT — patrol leader; later referred to as Captain.
2. Sepoy Arjun Ram.
3. Sepoy Bhanwar Lal Bhagaria (also spelled Bagaria in other accounts).
4. Sepoy Bhikaram (also rendered Bhika Ram).
5. Sepoy Moola Ram (also rendered Mula Ram).
6. Sepoy Naresh Singh.

The first build will not invent detailed private biographies or quotes. Each man should be visible, named in subtitles and the patrol roster, audible in normal squad interaction, and included equally in the memorial. Consult family or regimental representatives before a public release that uses likenesses, voices, or personal stories.

## 3. Historical setting for the mission

4 JAT had been in the Kargil sector since 1998, and Lt. Kalia joined the battalion in January 1999, according to the [regiment history](https://www.scribd.com/document/837922825/Jat-Regt-History-Vol-IV-Nov-2022). Bajrang had been vacated during heavy late-winter snow. The [Review Committee](https://www.nuclearweaponarchive.org/~nuclearw/India/KargilRCC.html) describes the wider intrusion as a stealthy advance through gaps in Indian winter positions. By the patrol date, suspicious movement and intrusions elsewhere were already known. The mission briefing should therefore ask the team to **check the Bajrang spur and report observations**, not discover the war for the first time.

The landscape is a steep, exposed spur off a larger ridge, with a glaciated valley, limited sightlines from below, loose rock, snow patches and wind. The camera should repeatedly establish the height difference between the patrol and its destination. The player should feel how a small ridge can dominate movement below without turning the setting into an endless whiteout. The exact walkable route remains reconstructed pending map and terrain review.

## 4. Mission flow and narrative beats

| Beat | Duration | Play and story | Evidence boundary |
|---|---:|---|---|
| 0. Context | 1–2 min | Opening title shows “Kaksar sector, May 1999.” Short sourced context: suspicious movement has been reported; Bajrang is unoccupied. | Dates and post status sourced; visuals composite. |
| 1. Briefing and roster | 3 min | Player controls Lt. Kalia. Meet the five men, check the patrol map and gear, hear the task in plain language. | Briefing dialogue reconstructed. No invented personal quotations. |
| 2. Valley approach | 5–7 min | Follow the patrol, learn cover, landmarks and hand signals. Distant ridge stays visible. Wind intermittently masks sound. | Route reconstructed; environmental conditions drawn from regional evidence. |
| 3. The climb | 6–8 min | Traverse scree, a rope-assisted rock section and exposed snow. Movement slows with exertion; sheltered pauses restore steadiness. Squad waits for one another. | Physical challenge evidence-based; exact obstacles designed for play. |
| 4. Reconnaissance | 3–5 min | Use binoculars to inspect a suspicious shape on the spur. Record an observation in the journal. No scripted radio claim that Kalia first reported the war. | The observation is a game construction, not a verified patrol record. |
| 5. Lost contact | 3–5 min | Weather, distance and hostile fire interrupt the patrol. A brief defensive encounter communicates how vulnerable a six-man patrol is below a held height. | Encounter is dramatized. No exact enemy count, speeches, or kill count. |
| 6. End and remembrance | 2–3 min | Fade out before capture or captivity. On-screen historical note states the patrol did not return and identifies the six; a short non-playable scene shows a search patrol going out. | Loss of contact, search and handover based on the regiment history. |

**Player objective:** complete the patrol’s reconnaissance steps and keep the team together during the climb. The historical outcome is fixed and is never presented as the player’s failure. Avoid “survive for X minutes” scoring, celebratory kill counters, and a retry screen that implies the six could simply have escaped if played better.

## 5. Mountain systems to prototype for this mission

1. **Readable vertical scale:** long views from the valley, then a closer ridge profile with clear ledges and exposure. The top should never feel like a short staircase.
2. **Effort and altitude:** slower sustained climb, heavy breathing, shorter sprint capacity, weapon steadiness affected by exertion. The character is already acclimatized; no fantasy oxygen depletion meter. [U.S. Army altitude guidance](https://api.army.mil/e2/c/downloads/2023/01/31/557d66e2/20-14.pdf).
3. **Surface feel:** rock, loose scree and snow give different footfall, traction and sound; route choice changes exertion and visibility.
4. **Visibility:** gusts can obscure parts of the slope for seconds, but landforms remain navigable. Offer a reduced-weather option.
5. **Squad presence:** six bodies are always accounted for through formation and check-in callouts; no squadmate silently teleports away.
6. **Small load:** rifle, limited magazines, binoculars, radio and cold-weather gear. Keep the interface legible and avoid invented technical specifications until equipment research is complete.
7. **Sound:** breath, wind on exposed rock, crunch underfoot, distant fire and radio interference create tension without constant music.

**Acceptance test:** In a playtest without a spoken explanation, a player should be able to point to the destination from the start, explain why the climb is difficult, name or recognize all six patrol members, and understand which events are documented versus reconstructed.

## 6. Visual and production constraints

Build the route first as simple geometry in Godot’s Compatibility renderer. On the detected GT 730, terrain silhouette, scale, sky, and sound will carry realism more effectively than expensive effects. Add LODs, baked lighting where suitable, adjustable render scale, and Performance/Balanced/High/Custom presets. No resolution or frame-rate promise until the first benchmark on this PC. [Godot system requirements](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.4/about/system_requirements.html).

The map is a **reconstruction**, informed by regional elevation data and historical descriptions. NASA SRTM can establish broad ridges but cannot resolve a climbable cliff or a 1999 post precisely. Hand-sculpt the route and mark the final map as interpretive. [NASA SRTM guide](https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-05/SRTM_Quick_Guide.pdf?VersionId=ZDrZprebSzTuJdC57vPsRXhuXU3S1OZK).

## 10. Prototype 0.1 technical and interpretation note (20 October 2026)

The current playable ridge is generated from a hand-authored height function: approximately 620 m of route and 118 m of nominal gain. No DEM, measured Bajrang approach, 1999 post plan, or witnessed patrol track has been applied. Cairns, shelter timing, the reconnaissance observation, squad positions, weather changes, and the encounter are explicitly game constructions. The route journal is a schematic player aid, not a recovered patrol record; its movement observation is fictional and marked as such. The exposed shelf and rope crossing between shelter and observation are gameplay obstacles; no reviewed source places rope equipment or that ledge on this patrol's route. The playable final-cairn exchange uses four generic high positions, temporary suppression, and a fixed duration solely as game design; no source verifies those numbers, positions, timing, or tactical actions. There is no kill count. The caption at the start and notes during play identify the reconstruction. The fixed ending names all six and stops before captivity.

The Godot 4.7.2 Compatibility renderer booted on the actual GT 730 via OpenGL 3.3. An automated collision check found walkable ground at 156 route samples and checked slope against the player controller limit. Actual GPU probes and the remaining performance uncertainty are recorded in the A–Z build log. This verifies a technical greybox, not the historical terrain or a full-length mission. Further research, playtests, and sensitivity review remain open before public release.
