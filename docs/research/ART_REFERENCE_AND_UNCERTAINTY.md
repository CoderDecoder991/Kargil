# Mission 1 art reference and uncertainty

## Direct turnaround character implementation — 24 October 2026

The patrol no longer renders `assets/models/patrol_soldier_generated.glb` or the former mesh/rifle rig. Gameplay uses the same generated character, texture and design shown in the user's high/low-ready sheets plus matching crouch sheets. Four additional project-generated walk-cycle sheets cover standing and crouched, high/low ready. Each is a 4-column by 8-row atlas, with four stride phases for each of eight directions. `tools/extract_turnaround_panels.py` crops and aligns frames, preserves the walk sheets' alpha, and fades the opaque grey backdrop from the original stand-pose turnarounds. The runtime frame phase advances by actual ground distance, with alternating footsteps; it no longer bobs the full sprite to imply movement. Crouch uses its own short, low walk cycle. These are 2D painted poses rather than joint animation or a 3D model; there is no climbing cycle, and the view steps between eight directions. The standing sprite height is 2.0 m only as a consistent gameplay scale; the sheets specify no real measurement. Generated kneeling and walking art is concept material, not historical imagery. All clothing, weapon details, face and poses remain unverified dramatized concept art and do not establish the six soldiers' real likeness, kit or loadout.


## Original character turnaround source and uncertainty — 24 October 2026

`assets/reference/rifle_high_ready_turnaround.png` and `assets/reference/rifle_low_ready_turnaround.png` are the two user-provided generated 4x2 character sheets. Their eight views are the direct runtime appearance and texture source for the standing high/low-ready patrol figures; they are not merely design references. Companion kneeling sheets `rifle_crouch_high_turnaround.png` and `rifle_crouch_low_turnaround.png` were generated for this project to retain the same character design in crouch. `tools/extract_turnaround_panels.py` cuts the 384x512 panels and fades the grey studio background for the 32 runtime sprites in `assets/Characters/generated_turnaround/`. Eight view sprites are selected based on camera direction, so this system reproduces the supplied art rather than physically rotating a 3D soldier. The nominal sprite height is 2.0 m for gameplay consistency, because the generated PNGs do not establish physical height. These generated images are not photographs, historical evidence, verified patrol kit, or likenesses of named people. The previous BAMEN-derived `patrol_soldier_generated.glb`, its Blender source, procedural soldier meshes, and separate patrol rifle are not used by the Indian patrol at runtime; they remain in the project for history/reference only. Enemy models remain separate.
## A05 visual pass

The visible patrol and opposing figures use five distinct patrol images (`patrol_armed_v1.png` through `_v5.png`) and four distinct opposing images (`opposing_armed_v1.png` through `_v4.png`) in `assets/Characters/`. These are project-generated AI concept cutouts, not historical photographs, likeness references, or verified depictions. Each character selects a different full-body, low-ready pose; the displayed figures remain camera-facing sprites with subtle whole-sprite movement and firing response. A side-angle inspection found the internal blockout rig too stylized to show, so the runtime deliberately keeps the cutouts active at all angles until production-quality side/back art or a reviewed skeletal model is ready. The images still have no true side/back views, articulated limbs, or skeletal animation. Clothing, faces, held weapons, and the opposing group are dramatized. The sheltered ledge windbreak is also a gameplay construction.

The isometric patrol figures carry an original, low-cost procedural AK-pattern prop. First person now uses the detailed textured AK mesh packaged with the credited gloved-arms asset; its authored grip marker drives the support-hand IK, with stock and spare magazine hidden outside the camera framing. This is a licensed art asset used as a generic visual reconstruction, not evidence of the patrol's equipment. The exact rifle carried by Lt. Kalia's patrol is not documented in the mission dossier. **Neither prop is presented as verified historical equipment or an identified patrol loadout.**

No historical photographs, uniforms, weapons, terrain models, or other third-party assets were copied into the game for this pass. The DRDO material was used as reference, not imported as art. The nine runtime armed variants are project-generated concepts; older user-provided concept images remain in the source asset folder but are not runtime character art. None of these images are likeness references, source evidence, or proof of period-correct clothing or weapons.

An original olive weave image was generated for this project and kept in `assets/textures/olive_fabric_source.png`. `scripts/build_fabric_texture.py` softens and reduces it to the 512 px `assets/textures/olive_fabric.png` used on clothing. The first raw treatment was rejected after a GT 730 screenshot showed high-frequency aliasing. The softer material improves surface variation but the figures still read as stylized at close range. No claim is made that this weave matches a documented 1999 uniform. The source and derived texture are project-created assets with no third-party license requirement.

The provided transparent `assets/weapons/insas_first_person_reference.png` cutout remains as visual reference and is no longer rendered in gameplay. Its side-on angle and missing hands made it unsuitable for the first-person view. The original 3D rifle and hand rig now rises through the lower edge on the right, with the muzzle directed toward screen center; holding right mouse aligns its iron sights, and R performs its visual magazine change. This is still simplified geometry and motion, not a production weapon rig. The traverse has thinner rope, dark anchor stakes, metal rings, and a coil. These forms are game reconstructions; no source locates such climbing hardware on the patrol's route.

The Government of India's [Kargil photo exhibition notice](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2036753&lang=2&reg=48) confirms that historical photographs, weapons, and equipment are displayed, but it does not establish the particular kit carried by Lt. Saurabh Kalia's patrol. The [National War Memorial Kargil overview](https://nationalwarmemorial.gov.in/war-depictions/details/8) provides broader historical context, not a specific visual reference for this six-person patrol.
